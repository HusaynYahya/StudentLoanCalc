    How the arithmetic works

    • Deductions look at the month, not the year. PAYE takes 9% of whatever that month's gross pay exceeds a twelfth of the annual threshold, rounded down to a whole pound. A month you earn nothing, you pay nothing, however good the rest of the year was.
    • Interest starts with the first instalment, in the September the course begins — not on graduation. Three years of that is why the balance on the first April is larger than the sum of what was borrowed.
    • Repayment begins the April after the course ends, and the write-off clock counts from that April, not from graduation.
    • Interest is charged monthly here, at a twelfth of the annual rate. The Student Loans Company charges it daily; over forty years the difference is a rounding error beside the uncertainty in RPI.
    • The write-off is unconditional. Whatever is left on the anniversary goes, however large. For most Plan 5 borrowers that, not the final payment, is how the loan ends.
    • An undergraduate plan and a postgraduate loan run side by side, each against its own threshold — 9% and 6%, so 15% across the band where both apply.

    What this cannot tell you

    Nothing here is a forecast. A forty-year projection turns entirely on RPI, on how fast the thresholds are uprated, and on a salary line nobody can know — and governments have changed all three, retrospectively, more than once. Treat it as a way of seeing how the rules behave under an assumption you have chosen, and change the assumption to see how much it mattered. For your own actual balance and plan type, sign in to your Student Loans Company repayment account; for the rules as they stand, read gov.uk/repaying-your-student-loan.
