The rules this follows

PlanWhoThresholdRateInterestWritten off
  • Deductions look at the month, not the year. 9% of whatever that month's gross pay exceeds a twelfth of the annual threshold, rounded down to a whole pound.
  • Interest starts with the first instalment, in the September the course begins — not on graduation. That is why the balance on the first April exceeds what was borrowed.
  • Repayment begins the April after the course ends, and the write-off clock counts from that April.
  • The write-off is unconditional. Whatever is left on the anniversary goes, however large. For most Plan 5 borrowers that, not a final payment, is how the loan ends.
  • Interest is charged monthly here, at a twelfth of the annual rate. The Student Loans Company charges it daily; over forty years that is a rounding error beside RPI.
  • An undergraduate plan and a postgraduate loan run side by side, each against its own threshold — 9% and 6%, so 15% where both bite.

Nothing here is a forecast. A forty-year projection turns entirely on RPI, on how fast the thresholds are uprated and on a salary nobody can know — and governments have changed all three, retrospectively, more than once.
